🇩🇪 Car sharing in Germany

Easy in big cities and at their airports

Four free-floating services in Berlin, two or three in the other big cities and cars at seven airports. With a non-EU licence, Free2move is the app service that says yes; Getaround's private cars do too.

In November, 3 days and 300 km from Berlin: car sharing €238 (MILES) against a typical rental at €328, so car sharing saves €90.

Airports

Berlin (BER)

Free2move, MILES, SIXT share and Bolt stand in car park P4 by Terminal 1, a few minutes' walk. Cars can only be taken and left there. MILES charges €8 to start and €8 to end.

Munich (MUC)

Free2move, MILES and SIXT share: car park P20, level 01, about 3 minutes' walk from Terminal 2. MILES: free to start, €12 to end.

Frankfurt (FRA)

Terminal 1, parking row 611. Follow the 'Carsharing' signs; the barrier opens by itself. Free2move €7.99 to start, €8.99 to end; MILES €5 to start, €4 to end.

Hamburg (HAM)

Free2move in car park P2/4, Area B (€7.99); SIXT share in car park P2. MILES serves the airport too, €9 each way.

Cologne, Düsseldorf, Stuttgart

Free2move and MILES serve these airports too. MILES charges €9 to start and €8 to end; Free2move shows its fee at booking.

Between cities and countries

  • Free2move: the trip ends in the city where it started. Bolt: some cars may end in other cities listed in the app, for a fee.
  • MILES: end in any of its 14 cities for €5.99. Berlin, Potsdam, Hamburg, Munich, Augsburg, Stuttgart, Frankfurt, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Duisburg, Wuppertal, Solingen, Mönchengladbach, Neuss.
  • SIXT share: end in another of its cities for €15, or at a SIXT branch for €39.
  • Abroad: MILES (11 neighbours, €5 a day, max €25) and SIXT share (€4.99) allow it, but the car must come back to Germany. Free2move only with its paid option (€16.99), Bolt only with consent.
  • cambio cars go back to their station, but may cross the EU, UK, Norway and Switzerland on the way.

Parking and fuel

  • Street parking in the business area is included with MILES and Free2move. MILES covers paid zones too.
  • Bolt: free in its marked areas; never end a trip behind a barrier, underground or in a private car park (marked airport bays excepted).
  • Fuel or charging is included with MILES and on Free2move minute and hour trips, not on Free2move or Bolt day rentals.
  • Car parks: end a trip only where the app or signs clearly allow it.

Before you go

  • Register in the app before you fly: licence, ID card or passport, and a bank card.
  • Licences: Free2move takes non-EU ones with an IDP or certified translation and proof of entry; Getaround takes foreign ones too. MILES needs an EU plastic-card licence and an EU address; Bolt and cambio take EU, EEA, UK and Swiss licences.
  • Station clubs are hard for visitors: DB Flinkster only takes people living in Germany, Austria or the Netherlands; stadtmobil wants a German registration certificate with a passport; book-n-drive takes EU licences only and runs a credit check; teilAuto needs a positive SCHUFA credit check.
  • Berlin and many other centres are low-emission zones (Umweltzone): every car needs a green sticker, foreign cars included. Coming in your own car? Order the sticker before you go.

Be ready for

  • Outside the big cities there is little for visitors: the station clubs want a German address, a credit check or an EU licence.
  • With a non-EU licence, Free2move is the only car-sharing app whose terms say yes (Getaround's private cars also do).
  • Free2move and MILES charge €500 for a car left outside the business area.
  • Day prices include few kilometres. Free2move's day includes 60 km.

Questions

Can I use car sharing in Germany with a licence from outside the EU?

3 of the 6 services open to visitors take licences from outside the EU in some form (Free2move, SIXT share and Getaround). MILES, Bolt Drive and cambio CarSharing take EU/EEA licences only. Each app checks your licence at sign-up; the table above says what each one accepts.

Do I need a credit card or a deposit?

MILES takes no deposit. Free2move, SIXT share, Bolt Drive and Getaround may hold an amount on your card or ask for a deposit; the cards above say what the terms publish. cambio CarSharing doesn't publish a deposit rule; the app shows it at sign-up. You add a bank card in the app and pay per trip. A rental desk usually wants a credit card in the driver's name and freezes a deposit.

Can I take a shared car out of Germany?

Free2move: may drive to Austria, Switzerland, France, Liechtenstein and Luxembourg, but the trip has to end in Germany. MILES: may drive to 11 other European countries, but the trip has to end in Germany. SIXT share: may drive to 25 other European countries, but the trip has to end in Germany. Bolt Drive: the trip has to end in Germany. cambio CarSharing: may drive to 35 other European countries, but the trip has to end in Germany. Getaround: not published, ask before you cross.

Is car sharing cheaper than a rental car in Berlin?

It depends on the trip. In November, one day and 60 km cost car sharing €70 (MILES) against a typical rental at €122, so car sharing saves €52. Three days and 300 km cost car sharing €238 (MILES) against a typical rental at €328, so car sharing saves €90. Check your own trip.

Can I pick up a car at the airport?

Berlin (BER): Free2move, MILES, SIXT share and Bolt stand in car park P4 by Terminal 1, a few minutes' walk. Cars can only be taken and left there. MILES charges €8 to start and €8 to end.
